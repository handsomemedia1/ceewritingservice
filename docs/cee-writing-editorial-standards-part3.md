# 30. THE "WHAT MOST BEGINNERS DON'T KNOW" LAYER

Every article must contain a layer of **expert insight that goes beyond the obvious explanation**.
A beginner can find basic definitions anywhere. CeeWriting should explain the things that a beginner is **unlikely to know until they have actually done research, supervised research, analyzed data, reviewed papers, or made mistakes themselves**.

Think: > **"What would an experienced researcher tell a beginner that a basic research-methods article probably wouldn't?"**

## THE 90% INSIGHT PRINCIPLE
Where genuinely applicable, every article should contain several insights that most beginners are unlikely to know. Do NOT manufacture a statistic claiming that "90% of researchers don't know this."
The actual goal is: > **Reveal the practical knowledge that is often missing from beginner explanations.**

### Examples of the type of insight we want:
* **Topic selection:** A beginner thinks a topic just needs to sound interesting. An expert knows it can fail due to inaccessible participants, unavailable data, ethical restrictions, inadequate sample size, etc.
* **Research questions:** A beginner thinks more questions = more comprehensive. An expert knows too many questions create an unmanageable study and fragmented analysis.
* **Literature review:** A beginner thinks they need as many papers as possible. An expert focuses on which studies matter, what they collectively show, where they disagree, and how methods differ.
* **Methodology:** A beginner chooses a method because "everyone uses it." An expert starts with the research question and type of evidence required.

# 31. THE "EXPERIENCED RESEARCHER WOULD TELL YOU..." TEST
Before completing every major section, ask: > **What would a highly experienced researcher add here that a textbook definition would not?**
Include insights natively without repetitive boxes (e.g., Hidden mistake, Practical reality, Decision rule, Warning, Nuance, Expert shortcut).

# 32. THE "THINGS I WISH SOMEONE TOLD ME EARLIER" LAYER
Where appropriate, include practical lessons usually learned through experience. (e.g., A research gap doesn't mean nobody has ever studied it; a statistically significant result does not automatically mean the finding is important).

# 33. THE "NEW AND USEFUL" LAYER
Identify newer developments, tools, practices, or changes that could genuinely help modern researchers (e.g., AI-assisted workflows, open science, preregistration).
**NEVER add something simply because it is new.** Does it actually make research better, easier, more transparent, more rigorous, or more accessible?

# 34. AI IN RESEARCH — BE PRACTICAL, NOT HYPE-DRIVEN
Explain what AI can help with (brainstorming, organizing, coding help) vs what it should NOT replace (critical reading, methodological judgment, ethical decisions). Be grounded and practical.

# 35. "BEYOND THE TEXTBOOK" STANDARD
Answer three levels of questions:
* Level 1 — What is it? (Beginner concept)
* Level 2 — How do I do it? (Practical process)
* Level 3 — What could go wrong, and what does an experienced researcher know? (The CeeWriting differentiator).

# 36. THE "WHAT CHANGES YOUR DECISION?" TEST
For every advanced insight, ask: > **Does knowing this change what the researcher would actually do?**
If yes, include it. If it merely sounds interesting, exclude it.

# 37. PREMIUM ARTICLE FORMULA
Where appropriate, structure important concepts around:
Basic explanation → Practical implementation → Common beginner mistake → What experienced researchers notice → What most beginners don't realize → What has changed/new developments → What to actually do.

# 38. THE FINAL EXPERT REVIEW
Before publishing, ask:
* "If an experienced researcher read this, would they say this tells beginners nothing new?" (If yes, improve it.)
* "If a complete beginner reads this, will they actually understand what to do?" (If no, simplify the explanation without simplifying the scholarship.)
**Target: Beginner accessibility + expert depth.**

# 39. THE CEEWRITING DIFFERENTIATOR
CeeWriting should be the place where a beginner can say: > "I understand what the textbook says. But CeeWriting also explains what actually happens when you try to do it."

The content should combine:
**Academic evidence + Experienced research reasoning + Practical workflow + Common mistakes + Hidden knowledge + Relevant new developments + Clear beginner-friendly teaching**
